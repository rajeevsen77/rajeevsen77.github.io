Biaxial Tensile Tester

Bai Research Group, Northeastern University
Jul 2026 — Present
Undergraduate Researcher

Converting a 3D-printed prototype tensile tester into a fully machined aluminum assembly, built to survive real test loads instead of just holding together for a demo.

The first gripper joint I built passed initial checks, then toppled under load — a center-of-gravity and moment problem I hadn't accounted for. I used SolidWorks Motion Study to find where it was actually failing, then evaluated three redesigns (dovetail, T-slot, and a modified pin joint) against machining limits, cost, and size. The dovetail below was one of the three — ultimately dropped once its feature sizes turned out to be impractical to machine reliably at this scale.

After consulting two senior machinists on feasibility, cost, and size constraints, I moved forward with a modified pin joint design integrated into the tester's sliding gripper assembly and linear guide rails.

The machine-shop-ready drawings went out with critical dimensions toleranced to ±0.005", and the first machined aluminum components are back from the shop.

Introduction to Airframe Course

AeroNU, Northeastern Aerospace Club
Sep — Dec 2025
Motor ignition, St. Albans, VT — Dec 2025

Designed, simulated, and built a 6-foot J-class rocket airframe using OpenRocket and SolidWorks, then fabricated it with laser cutting, hand tools, and epoxy.

Less than a week before launch, the motor retainer — epoxied in place and impossible to simply swap out — hit a curb during a transport mishap and bent. I designed and 3D-printed a replacement retainer with a metal clamp plate on short notice. The rocket flew to 3,000 feet, within 5% of the target apogee.

Project Albatross

AeroNU, Northeastern Aerospace Club — Co-Lead
Aug 2026 — Present
Early concept sketch — folded-wing glider
Reference image, NASA archival — Rogallo wing test, not our work. Used as a research reference for guided-descent concepts.

Our rockets come down under a standard parachute, which means the landing spot is mostly up to the wind — sometimes that's a farm field, sometimes it's the treeline at the edge of one. There's currently no way to steer that.

I'm co-leading a new initiative with another sophomore to design a guided-descent glider recovery system that can actually be steered to a landing zone. We're in the early concept phase now — defining what the system actually needs to do and reviewing early design directions as a team, with a target of a prototype glider and deployment mechanism by the end of 2026.

SteadySip Smart Mug

Cornerstone of Engineering, Northeastern University
Jan — Apr 2026
Finished prototype, LED feedback active

Designed an assistive mug for people with hand tremors: a double-walled 3D-printed body, a bayonet-mount electronics compartment, and a handle that pivots 90 degrees on a simple screw-washer-nut joint while staying structurally secure.

Programmed a Raspberry Pi Pico in MicroPython to read a thermistor and a reed switch, driving dual RGB LEDs to give real-time feedback on temperature and whether the lid is properly seated. Getting the wire routing and wall thickness right took several rounds of reprinting.
